# SPEC-070: Ownership leaves the hash, and an approved-set snapshot carries the alarm

> Implements **DR-088 (ownership leaves the hash; the union of the approved and current
> owned sets carries the alarm)**, `status: accepted`, via **#1800 (the implementing
> issue)**. DR-088 carries the rationale, the measurements and the two costs; this spec is
> the build. Tier **T4 (complete ceremony)** because the change is a content contract
> embedded in 61 committed records, mirrored across two language implementations, one of
> which MinSpec scaffolds into other people's repositories.

## One-Sentence Scope

Take `implements:`, `affects:` and `implements_reason:` out of the canonical approval
hash, and replace the alarm those lines were carrying with a snapshot of the **structured**
owned set recorded at approve time, seeded onto every existing approved record by a
migration, and a spec-gate freeze scoped to the union of the approved and current sets.

## Context

### Why the backfill currently costs a real sign-off

`canonicalizeSpec` strips exactly two frontmatter keys, `status` and `phases`
(`packages/shared/src/canonical.ts:60-83`, `stripLifecycle`; the Python twin does the same
at `scripts/hooks/canonical.py:37-53`). Everything else in frontmatter is hashed content,
so an `implements:` line is inside the bytes the human signed. Adding one to an approved
spec moves `specHash`, the record resolves `stale` (`approval.ts:483-489`), and only a
human can restore it.

SPEC-038 FR-3 requires the declaration past Clarify, and approval is what advances `plan`
to `in-progress`, so the requirement arms at the moment the signature is minted. SPEC-051
INV-2 (approval integrity) named the escape and refused to take it without a decision:
*"If a Clarify option chooses to exclude `implements:` from the canonical hash (Option B
below), that is a change to the DR-034 hashing contract and requires its own DR and
explicit human sign-off"* (`specs/minspec/SPEC-051-ownership-before-approval/requirements.md:138`).
DR-088 is that DR, accepted 2026-09-09.

### What the hash was actually buying, and what it was not

Hashing ownership never blocked an ownership edit. A spec's own markdown is permanently
unownable (`.md` is not in `_SRC_EXT_RE`, `scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py:293-295`), and an
approved spec blocks nothing at all - only `unapproved` and `stale` specs enter the
blocking set (`spec-gate.py:512-513`). What it bought was that **rewriting the ownership
claim was loud and self-punishing**: the edit staled the approval and the human re-read
before signing again. After the strip that edit is silent, with the human's name still
behind it. DR-088 section 2 (the replacement alarm) exists to restore the loudness by a
different mechanism - with one measured exception this spec now names explicitly, the
`implements: none` cohort (FR-6, Risks).

### The control that was tried first, and why it failed

This survives into the spec because the failure shapes the design. DR-088's first draft
proposed a **digest** over the ownership lines, arming the spec-gate with an
`ownership.drifted` verdict. Two review rounds plus measurement killed it on two counts:

1. **Reach.** The gate skips any spec whose phase intent is not `implementing`/`done`, at
   the `continue` now at `scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py:501`, and that skip runs *before* the
   owned set is computed at `:510`. Re-measured at commit `719878d6`: of the approved
   specs with the `implements:` key absent, **2 are reachable and 18 are skipped** (17 of
   the 18 carry no `phases:` block at all; the remaining one, SPEC-022, is `specifying`).
2. **Direction.** A digest proves the declaration changed but cannot reconstruct what it
   was, so the freeze could only be scoped from the post-edit declaration. `owned_file_set`'s
   own docstring states the consequence: *"A spec that declares NO impl files owns NOTHING
   and therefore blocks nothing"* (`spec-gate.py:377-388`). Rewriting `implements:` to
   `none` produced an **empty** freeze, in exactly the case the control existed to catch.

The replacement records the owned **set** rather than a digest of it, and freezes on the
union of the approved and current sets. A list can answer "what did the signature cover";
a digest cannot.

**The empty-freeze failure is a shape, not an instance, and it recurs.** Adversarial
review of this spec's first draft found it reproducing in three places at once: in a
no-snapshot fallback that scoped the freeze from the post-edit declaration (FR-8), in a
migration that wrote no snapshot at all so that fallback governed the entire corpus (FR-9),
and in a union rule scoped to approved records only, so that staling a record first
released the approved set (FR-6). Each is closed below, and each has a mirror-of-AC-8
acceptance criterion, because the one assertion the first design lacked is the one that
would have caught it.

### Ambiguity that must be resolved before any count is used

"Declares no `implements:`" has two readings and they differ by thirteen specs.
`implements: none` **is** a declaration (it satisfies the validator escape at
`spec-validator.ts:807`) but owns nothing at the gate, because `consider` rejects a token
with no `/` (`spec-gate.py:343-344`). DR-088's `24`/`20` is the **key-absent** reading. Of
the 34 in-band spec files here, 19 declare a real path, 13 declare `implements: none`, and
2 have the key absent. Every requirement and acceptance criterion below states which
reading it means.

## Functional Requirements

- **FR-1 (BINDING PRECONDITION - the strip must not ship first, and must not ship without
  the migration).** The widened strip (FR-2) MUST NOT land on `main` before
  `ownedAtApproval` is **written, read, and seeded onto the existing corpus**: FR-4 (the
  record field, including the producer), FR-5 (the computation), FR-6 (the union freeze),
  FR-8 (the missing-witness verdict) **and FR-9 (the migration, including FR-9.10's
  seeding)** must all be in effect in the same merged state or an earlier one. This is not
  sequencing preference; it is irreversibility plus a measured repo-wide freeze.

  *Rationale, part one - irreversibility.* Once ownership lines are outside the hash and no
  snapshot has been recorded, git history can no longer resolve what a signature covered,
  because **every commit differing only in the `implements:` key hash-matches**. A reviewer
  cannot recover the approved set from the record (it was never written), from the hash (it
  no longer distinguishes), or from history (every candidate revision verifies). The loss
  is not "drift becomes invisible" but "drift becomes unrecoverable".

  *Rationale, part two - the transitional freeze, measured.* The strip alone stales every
  record whose hash moves. Measured at `719878d6`: **22** in-band T3/T4 records with a
  non-empty owned set go stale the instant the widened strip lands, and a stale record
  enters the gate's blocking set (`spec-gate.py:512-513`), freezing **88** distinct source
  files until the migration runs. FR-9.8 observes that the house-precedent migration is
  wired into no npm script and no workflow, so "it ran" would otherwise be an assumption
  rather than a signal. A merged state that satisfies FR-2 without FR-4 through FR-9 is a
  defect even if every test passes, and the Plan MUST state how the ordering is enforced
  rather than intended.

- **FR-2 (the strip, in all three copies of the canonicalizer, plus the one consumer that
  is neither).** `stripLifecycle` MUST drop `implements`, `affects` and `implements_reason`
  alongside `status` and `phases`, applying the **`phases:` indented-children rule** to all
  three, and the change MUST land simultaneously in:
  1. `packages/shared/src/canonical.ts` (`stripLifecycle`, `:60-83`);
  2. `scripts/hooks/canonical.py` (`_strip_lifecycle`, `:37-53`);
  3. the base64 `CANONICAL_PY` in `packages/minspec/src/lib/ci-review-templates.ts:3072`,
     regenerated with `node scripts/gen-ci-templates.mjs` and **never hand-edited**. Rule 12
     (FATAL) at `scripts/validate-frontmatter.ts:522-557` fails `npm run validate` in CI's
     lint job on a stale embed; it iterates `gen.generateAll(ROOT)`, so it covers every
     generated file including `APPROVAL_PROVENANCE_PY` (`ci-review-templates.ts:2899`).

  **A fourth artifact consumes this basis and is not a canonicalizer:
  `scripts/approval-provenance.py`.** It imports `spec_hash` from the scaffolded
  `canonical.py` (`approval-provenance.py:50`) and recomputes the hash of the spec at head
  (`:182`) and at base (`:202`) to rule on whether an approval is backed; `scripts/review-branch.sh:107-108`
  invokes it on every review, and its output is the TRUSTED machine-generated block the AI
  review panel rules on. Between a strip landing and a migration re-stamping, it reports
  `VERDICT: MISMATCH - the record does NOT match its spec (a real finding)` on records that
  are perfectly valid, and its `was_stale` line mis-reports old-basis records as already
  stale at base. That is the false forged-sign-off block the script was written to prevent
  (its own docstring cites PR #1017). FR-1 closes this window **in this repo** by requiring
  the strip and the migration in one merged state; it does **not** close it for adopters,
  who receive the widened `canonical.py` and no migration - see FR-11 and OQ-2.

  Two implementation traps are normative, not advisory:
  - **Use the `phases:` branch, not the `status:` branch.** `PHASES_LINE_RE` sets
    `inPhasesBlock = true`; `STATUS_LINE_RE` merely `continue`s, so a `status:` key with
    indented children leaves orphan child lines in the hash (measured, identical in both
    twins: `status:\n  value: x\n` canonicalizes to `  value: x` retained). DR-088's "same
    indented-children rule" means the `phases:` rule.
  - **The block terminates on the first NON-indented line, and a blank line counts as
    non-indented.** Measured in both twins: a `phases:` block with an internal blank line
    leaks its remaining children into the hash. Zero corpus specs hit this today, so it is
    latent for `phases:` - but authors visually separate ownership lists far more readily
    than a tool-written `phases:` map, so extending the same rule makes it live. The Plan
    MUST state whether the blank-line behaviour is preserved or fixed, and a fixture MUST
    pin whichever is chosen, in both twins.

- **FR-3 (the owned set must be parsed correctly before it is signed).** Before any
  `ownedAtApproval` value is written, the resolution of `implements:`/`affects:` MUST
  either (a) handle block-form YAML lists in full, in both twins, or (b) reject block form
  at write time with an actionable error, so no truncated set can be signed. Silently
  signing a first-item-only set is forbidden. *Rationale:* #1961 (block-form ownership
  lists truncated to their first item) is live in the corpus today; a snapshot taken
  through it records a set the author never declared, and the union in FR-6 then reads the
  missing items as ownership **removal** on the next parser fix. Which of (a) or (b) is a
  Clarify decision (OQ-3).

  **Option (b) is incompatible with OQ-4's recommended ordering and the Plan must not
  choose both.** Measured at `719878d6`: SPEC-058, SPEC-059, SPEC-060 and SPEC-061 all use
  block-form `affects:`, all four are approved and hash-fresh, and all four are on FR-10's
  correction list. OQ-4 ordering (ii) has each correction stale a freshly re-stamped record
  and require a human re-approval afterwards - which option (b) would refuse, because
  `MinSpec: Approve Spec` would reject their block-form declarations. A rejecting
  implementation MUST therefore first rewrite the 13 block-form declarations to inline form,
  and the Plan MUST say who does that and when.

- **FR-4 (`ownedAtApproval` on the record - write side AND read side, same slice).**
  `ApprovalRecord` MUST carry `ownedAtApproval?: string[]` (repo-relative POSIX paths), and
  **all six** of the following MUST change together:
  1. the interface, `packages/minspec/src/lib/approval.ts:60-69`;
  2. `isValidRecord`, `packages/minspec/src/lib/approval-store.ts:96-112` - **optional**;
     absent MUST remain valid. A required check drops every one of the 61 committed
     records, and a dropped record reads `unapproved`, which arms the gate against the whole
     corpus. The in-code warning at `approval-store.ts:89-94` **states that failure mode as
     a hypothetical and forbids the required-string shape** ("a required-string check here
     **would** silently drop every existing approval… Instead: absent is VALID"); it is not
     a report of a past incident. 4 sidecars still lack `baselineBlob` today;
  3. `normalizeRecord`, `approval-store.ts:121-136` - **the whitelist trap**. It is a
     whitelist constructor, not a spread: it rebuilds the record from seven named fields
     plus one conditional, and there is no `...r`. `isValidRecord` does not reject unknown
     keys, so a sidecar carrying `ownedAtApproval` is **valid today**, is read as a record,
     and the field is discarded one function later with no error, no warning and no dropped
     record. Every read path funnels through it (`readRecord:148-153`, `listRecords:179-195`,
     `listOrphanedRecords:223-240`); there is no `readRawRecord`;
  4. the **producer**, `approveSpec`'s record literal at `approval.ts:581-590`. This is a
     separate failure from (3) and is the one that actually happened: `reviewStart` is fully
     plumbed on the read side (validated at `approval-store.ts:110`, carried at `:132-134`,
     with a passing test at `approve-baseline.test.ts:146-155`) and is populated in **0 of
     61** sidecars (verified here) because `approval.ts:588` omits it, 76 days after
     **SPEC-017 Slice 2 (extend `ApprovalRecord` with `baselineBlob` plus a back-compat
     validator)** declared it in commit `536e051d`. DR-088's mitigation text names only
     the three read-side seams and a round-trip test; none of those would have caught the
     failure it cites;
  5. the **second producer**, `scripts/migrate-approvals.ts:53-60`, which carries its own
     duplicated `ApprovalRecord` interface (already missing `baselineBlob`) and two
     from-scratch literals at `:270-277` and `:293-300`. Records it writes validate and are
     thinner;
  6. the **trusted consumer**, `scripts/approval-provenance.py`. `_sidecar_fields`
     (`:100-108`) returns `(specPath, specHash)` and nothing else, so the report is
     structurally blind to `ownedAtApproval`. After FR-2 the ownership lines are outside the
     hash, so a PR that rewrites a sidecar's `ownedAtApproval` to `[]` **and** the spec's
     `implements:` to `none` leaves `specHash` unchanged, and the trusted block affirmatively
     reports `VERDICT: MATCHES` plus `previous record: same specHash (only metadata such as
     approvedAt changed)` (`:196`) - telling the reviewer, in machine-generated text they are
     instructed to trust, that nothing substantive changed at the exact moment the freeze the
     signature bought was zeroed. `.minspec/approvals/` is inside the auto-mergeable docs
     corpus (`.github/workflows/docs-lane.yml:84`), so no second human necessarily reads the
     diff. The report MUST therefore diff `ownedAtApproval` between base and head and state
     whether the spec's resolved ownership changed, and the `:196` line MUST branch so a
     genuinely metadata-only change reads differently from an ownership-snapshot change.
     The file is a managed template (`template-registry.ts:2179-2185`, `content:
     APPROVAL_PROVENANCE_PY`), so the change also regenerates its embed (FR-2 rule 3).

  `approveSpec` never reads the prior sidecar and `writeRecord` is a whole-file overwrite
  (`approval-store.ts:160-164`), so a value present on disk but absent from the literal is
  **erased** on the next approve, not merged.

- **FR-5 (a TypeScript owned-set computation that equals the gate's, and a snapshot scoped
  to the STRUCTURED declaration).** A single exported function MUST compute the owned set in
  TypeScript and MUST agree with `owned_file_set`/`declared_impl_files` (`spec-gate.py:306-388`)
  on the whole corpus, for **both** the structured and the fuzzy signal. **No such function
  exists today**: `grep -rn 'ownedFileSet|declaredImplFiles|owned_file_set' --include='*.ts'`
  returns nothing. The only TypeScript mirror is `isValidOwnedPath`
  (`packages/minspec/src/lib/ownership-path-rules.ts:44-60`), which mirrors `consider()`
  **minus** the existence check and has no counterpart for the fuzzy `tasks.md` backtick arm.

  **What `ownedAtApproval` snapshots is the STRUCTURED set only** (`implements:` +
  `affects:`, resolved through `consider()`'s precision filters, `require_exists=False`).
  The full `owned_file_set` - structured plus fuzzy - remains the gate's **freeze scope**;
  it is only the snapshot and the FR-6 drift comparison that are structured-only. Three
  reasons, each measured:
  - The structured set is what the signature covered. `canonicalizeSpec` hashes **one file**;
    `tasks.md` is not inside `requirements.md`'s hashed bytes, so a snapshot including fuzzy
    paths would mint a witness for paths no signature ever covered - manufacturing a
    sign-off nobody gave, in a different currency.
  - The full set is non-stationary. A path enters or leaves it when a file named in a
    `tasks.md` backtick is created or deleted, or when `tasks.md` is edited. Under plain set
    inequality that is drift, so the union freeze would fire on ordinary implementation
    progress: an implementer creates a file `tasks.md` already names, the current set grows,
    and the freeze blocks them from editing the file they just created until a human acks.
  - It would deadlock this spec. `SPEC-017/requirements.md` declares nothing structurally
    and owns 22 files through the fuzzy arm, three of which are in this spec's `affects:`
    list. Under a full-set witness rule, FR-7 plus FR-8 freeze them before this spec's own
    implementation can start.

  This requirement **creates a new Node-equals-Python parity obligation**. DR-088 section 2
  constraint 4 claims the set design avoids one; that claim is correct about *hashing* (a
  path list needs no canonicalization) and incorrect about *set computation*, because the
  gate now compares a TypeScript-written snapshot against its own Python-computed set. The
  DR needs amending on this point (Follow-ups). Two known divergences must be closed or
  explicitly accepted:
  - `fmListField` strips an inline `# comment` before tokenizing; the gate's `fm_value`
    keeps comment tokens. The asymmetry is deliberate and documented at
    `spec-validator.ts:735-744` as fail-safe while the two feed independent decisions. Under
    a union comparison it yields a **permanent false `ownership-drifted`** on any spec whose
    ownership line carries an inline comment. Zero corpus hits today; live the moment a
    backfill writes an explanatory inline comment naming a file.
  - The gate merges `implements` and `affects` into one indistinguishable flat set
    (`spec-gate.py:362-364` into `:339`/`:359`) while the validator counts `implements`
    alone (`spec-validator.ts:806`) and uses `affects` only for an escaping-path check
    (`:825`). A spec declaring only `affects:` fully arms the gate and still trips
    `ownership.implements.missing`.

- **FR-6 (the union freeze, under an `ownership-drifted` verdict, for any record carrying a
  snapshot).** For a record whose `ownedAtApproval` is present and differs from the spec's
  current **structured** owned set, the spec-gate MUST deny writes to
  `union(ownedAtApproval, current owned_file_set)`. Three scoping rules are normative:

  1. **The rule binds on the presence of a snapshot, not on the approval verdict.** A
     `stale` or `unapproved` record that carries `ownedAtApproval` contributes
     `union(ownedAtApproval, current)` to `blocking`, not the current set alone. Without
     this, two ordinary edits release the approved set: edit the spec body so the record
     goes `stale`, then rewrite `implements:` to `none`; the pre-existing path at
     `spec-gate.py:512-513` appends the **current** set (`:510`), which is now empty, and a
     rule scoped to approved records never consults the snapshot. 3 of 61 sidecars are
     stale today, so the path is live, not theoretical.
  2. **Removing a declaration from a spec that still exists MUST freeze what the signature
     covered rather than release it.** The qualifier is honest, not defensive: the gate
     enumerates only spec files present on disk (`spec-gate.py:450`) and an orphaned sidecar
     is never read, so `rm specs/.../requirements.md` removes the declaration, the record
     and the freeze in one step - and `rm` is not `Edit`/`Write`/`MultiEdit`, so the hook
     never runs at all (`spec-gate.py:412`). `listOrphanedRecords` exists
     (`approval-store.ts:223`) and is consulted by `scripts/validate-frontmatter.ts:446`,
     never by the gate. Closing that is deferred (Out of Scope) and the claim is scoped
     accordingly rather than stated as an absolute the code does not hold.
  3. **Every freeze stays scoped to one spec's own files** by `owned_match`
     (`spec-gate.py:391-401`), never repo-wide. An empty `ownedAtApproval` against an empty
     current structured set is **not** drift.

  **One cohort the replacement alarm cannot cover, stated rather than implied.** For a spec
  whose declaration is `implements: none`, both sets are permanently empty, so drift is
  structurally inert - while FR-2 removes `implements_reason:` from the hash, and that field
  is a validator input, not prose: the `none` escape is satisfied only when it is present
  (`spec-validator.ts:807`). The escape's justification can therefore be rewritten under the
  human's signature with neither the hash nor the drift check able to notice. Measured: **13
  of the 34 in-band T3/T4 spec files declare `implements: none`**. DR-088 selected
  `implements_reason` for the strip on its own separate argument, which this spec does not
  re-litigate; whether the sidecar also records the reason so drift can see it is OQ-10.

- **FR-7 (evaluated above the phase band, per record).** Drift MUST be evaluated before the
  phase-intent `continue` at `spec-gate.py:501`. DR-088 says `rec` and the approval verdict
  are already in hand there, which is true (`:472`, `:478-483`) and incomplete. **Two**
  things move:
  1. the owned-set call itself. `files = owned_file_set(cwd, sp, fm)` is at `:510`, **nine
     lines below** the `continue`. Implement the record literally and you get
     `ownedAtApproval` with no current set to union against;
  2. a `drifted` accumulator parallel to `blocking` (`:512-513`), with its own `owned_match`
     pass and deny branch. The existing `matched` computation at `:519-520` and the deny at
     `:538-545` read `blocking` only, and the fail-closed `canon_resolved` guard at
     `:529-536` must state explicitly whether it covers drift.

  **Drift is evaluated per RECORD, which is per spec FILE, and is order-independent by
  construction.** Approval records are path-keyed (`approval-store.ts:79`, `sidecarPath` is a
  pure function of the repo-relative spec path; `spec-gate.py:472` resolves one per file) and
  each file's owned set is computed from that file's own frontmatter (`:510`). Nothing about
  the drift verdict needs a representative file per spec id, and because FR-7 evaluates
  **above** the `continue` at `:500-501` it is also above the `seen_ids` dedup at `:505-507` -
  so the dedup never applies to it and **nothing needs to be moved**. The dedup MUST be left
  where it is: moving it above the band would let an out-of-band sibling claim a spec id and
  then be dropped at the band, removing an in-band unapproved sibling's contribution to
  `blocking` entirely, which is a fail-open direction under constitution invariant 2.

  **Why this is a requirement and not an optimisation:** leave the evaluation below the
  band and the drift control reaches 2 of the 20 approved key-absent specs and skips 18 -
  the same failure that killed the digest design, reproducing on its replacement. FR-8
  additionally becomes inert for those 18, which is a silent gate under constitution
  invariant 2. The measured cost of the widening is 12 spec ids / 50 files (above).

- **FR-8 (`ownership.witness.missing` fails closed and visibly, and never scopes a freeze
  from the post-edit declaration).** Three normative clauses:

  1. **Trigger, stated without reference to the current declaration.** A record that is
     `approved` and carries **no** `ownedAtApproval`, or carries one that is present but not
     an array of strings, is a **missing witness**. The trigger MUST NOT be conditioned on
     the spec currently declaring ownership: that precondition is false the moment someone
     rewrites `implements:` to `none`, so conditioning on it makes the verdict unreachable in
     exactly the removal case it exists to catch. Malformed MUST be treated as missing, never
     coerced: `read_record` shape-checks only `specHash` (`spec-gate.py:277`), so a value of
     `"packages/x/a.ts"`, `{}` or `null` reaches the comparison, and the natural
     `set(rec.get("ownedAtApproval") or [])` turns a bare string into a set of single
     characters that `owned_match`'s lower-casing can never match (`:391-401`), yielding the
     empty freeze again through a malformed witness. The TypeScript `isValidRecord` addition
     drops such a record entirely, so the two twins MUST be specified to agree on which
     values are malformed and on the resulting verdict.
  2. **Consequence at the gate.** A missing witness is **non-approving**. The record
     contributes to `blocking`, the denial reason names the missing witness, and absence MUST
     NOT be silently tolerated or reported only as a warning. Stated honestly: with no
     snapshot and a post-edit declaration of `none`, the gate has nothing to freeze, which is
     why clause 3 exists. The freeze scope MUST NOT be defined as "the current declaration
     taken as the whole set" - that is the empty freeze DR-088 section 2 records as having
     killed the digest design.
  3. **Consequence in the corpus - the gate that makes clause 2's hard case unreachable.** A
     standing check MUST fail `npm run validate` (error, not warning) on any approved record
     that lacks a well-formed `ownedAtApproval`. Combined with FR-9.10, which seeds every
     old-fresh record, this makes "approved with no witness" an un-committable state rather
     than a state the write-gate must somehow recover from. This is the RCDD Phase 4 move:
     the bad state a gate should have rejected gets the gate, instead of a fallback that
     tries to guess what the signature covered.

  *Rationale:* constitution invariant 2 - a missing or errored witness fails the gate closed
  and visibly. **Scope:** FR-8 binds **this repo's** gate and this repo's validator. Adopter
  behaviour is OQ-2, and resolving OQ-2 toward a warn default requires **amending FR-8**,
  not merely answering the question. Whether clause 1 is one verdict with `ownership-drifted`
  or two is OQ-1.

- **FR-9 (the migration).** A migration MUST re-stamp and seed the corpus, with these
  properties, each independently testable:
  1. **Classify before writing.** Every sidecar, under both bases, with nothing written
     until the whole tree is classified.
  2. **Frozen, fixture-pinned old canonicalizer.** The old hash MUST come from a vendored

     "Fixture-pinned" is the second half: a copy that is merely copied re-diverges the next
     time someone fixes it, and `canonical.ts` has already absorbed one silent basis change
     (the #252 relative-link collapse, `canonical.ts:115`).
  3. **Re-stamp only records fresh under the OLD basis.** The new canonical form is the old
     one minus the ownership lines, so its coverage is a strict subset; re-stamping an
     old-fresh record asserts nothing the human did not already sign. Measured: 36 records.
  4. **Quarantine, never silently keep, any record whose status would improve** from
     `stale`/`unapproved` to `approved` - **with the mechanism named and one exemption.**
     - *Mechanism.* Quarantine MUST be represented by a change the readers actually see.
       `resolveStatus` consults nothing but the hash (`approval.ts:483-489`),
       `normalizeRecord` is a whitelist constructor with no `...r` (`approval-store.ts:121-136`)
       and `isValidRecord` does not reject unknown keys (`:96-112`), and the Python reader
       shape-checks only `specHash` (`spec-gate.py:276-278`). A marker such as `quarantined:
       true` written alongside an untouched `specHash` therefore produces a record that still
       resolves `approved` in **both** twins while looking handled. The representation MUST
       move the stored hash out of `specHash` (for example to `quarantinedHash`, leaving
       `specHash` absent or a sentinel) so the record is non-approving by the only mechanism
       either reader has, and MUST preserve `approvedBy`/`approvedAt` - deletion destroys the
       provenance the human did produce. Every field this introduces is subject to FR-4's
       six-site rule.
     - *Exemption.* A record carrying `rehashedAt` is **excluded from the improve
       classification**. It is a known artifact of this migration, not a laundered sign-off:
       a re-stamped record carries the NEW-basis hash, so on any resumed or second run it
       computes stale under the frozen old basis and fresh under the new - the exact "would
       improve" signature - and without this exemption run 2 quarantines all 36 re-stamped
       records while FR-9.6 and AC-15 promise it writes nothing. INV-3 names this phenomenon
       for a standing check and it applies identically to the migration's own second run.
  5. **Provenance:** a re-stamp adds `rehashedAt` and `rehashedFrom` and MUST NOT touch
     `migrated`. `migrated` means "an approval the human never performed"; it resolves to
     `approved` everywhere (`resolveStatus` reads only the hash, `approval.ts:483-489`) so
     it would not block anything, but DR-034's warn-to-error ratchet requires zero migrated
     records and the corpus is clean today (0 of 61). Both new fields are subject to FR-4's
     six-site rule.
  6. **Idempotency keyed on `rehashedAt`, not on file existence.** The house precedent,
     `scripts/migrate-approvals.ts:118-120`, achieves idempotency by `written.has(rel) ||
     existsSync(sidecarPath(rel))` - path presence, never record correctness. Copied
     literally, a run that crashes midway skips every already-written record on retry and
     every not-yet-written one for the wrong reason.
  7. **Iterate the sidecar tree, never `byId`.** `representativeById`
     (`migrate-approvals.ts:219-229`) collapses files to one representative per id, and 3 of
     the 61 committed sidecars are unreachable that way - two of them among the 3 stale
     records. Note also that `packages/minspec/src/lib/spec-catalog.ts:77-79` asserts
     `specs/minspec/{requirements,design,tasks}.md` are SPEC-001/002/003; all three carry
     `id: SPEC-001` at `719878d6`. Read frontmatter, not the comment.
  8. **A real failure exit code, a post-write read-back, and a wired entry point.** The
     precedent has no `process.exit`, no `throw`, and six bare `catch {}` swallow-blocks, so
     a partial run is indistinguishable from a clean one. It is also wired into no npm script
     and no workflow. This migration MUST be reachable from `package.json` and MUST emit a
     signal a check can read, because FR-1 makes "the migration ran" a merge precondition.
  9. **Do not read the gitignored legacy store.** `/home/jason/code/MinSpecPro/.minspec/approvals.json`
     still exists with 16 records, and `canonicalMinspecDir` resolves through
     `--git-common-dir` (`migrate-approvals.ts:72-85`), so a run launched from a worktree
     reads the primary checkout's copy while writing sidecars into the worktree. The new
     migration MUST NOT inherit that resolution.
  10. **Seed `ownedAtApproval` on every record fresh under the OLD basis, including the ones
      it does not re-stamp.** This is the property the first draft dropped, and without it
      the whole design is inert: 0 of 61 sidecars carry the field today, so every approved
      record would enter FR-8's missing-witness branch on day one, and INV-4 would be false
      for the entire corpus on the day the change lands.
      - *Value.* The **structured** owned set (FR-5) resolved at the migration commit, sorted
        and deduped; the **empty array** where the spec declares nothing structurally, which
        is the honest statement DR-088's "grandfathered 24" paragraph prescribes for that
        cohort ("the signature covered a spec that declared nothing").
      - *Licence.* Old-basis freshness is the proof: it establishes that the frontmatter
        bytes, `implements:` included, are unchanged since the human signed, so the current
        declaration **is** what the signature covered. A record that is **not** old-fresh MUST

      - *Scope.* All 58 old-fresh records, not only the 36 that need a re-stamp. The 22

      - *Day-one drift population.* Zero by construction: every seeded record's snapshot

## Contract

```ts
// packages/minspec/src/lib/approval.ts - additive, all optional.
export interface ApprovalRecord {
  readonly specPath: string;
  readonly specHash: string;
  readonly approvedAt: string;
  readonly approvedBy: string;
  readonly tier: Tier;
  readonly migrated: boolean;
  readonly baselineBlob: string;
  readonly reviewStart?: string;
  /** FR-4 + FR-5. Resolved STRUCTURED owned set (`implements:` + `affects:`) at approve
   *  time, repo-relative POSIX, sorted, deduped. NOT the fuzzy tasks.md contribution.
   *  Absent OR malformed = no witness (FR-8.1). Present-and-empty = "the signature
   *  covered a spec that declared nothing", which is a real, distinct state. */
  readonly ownedAtApproval?: readonly string[];
  /** FR-9.5. Written only by the migration. Also the FR-9.4 improve-classification
   *  exemption key. */
  readonly rehashedAt?: string;
  readonly rehashedFrom?: string;
}
```

`isValidRecord` addition (FR-4.2), written so absence stays valid for all 61 existing
records:

```ts
(r.ownedAtApproval === undefined ||
  (Array.isArray(r.ownedAtApproval) && r.ownedAtApproval.every((x) => typeof x === 'string')))
```

**The Python gate needs no schema *addition*, but it does need a normative *consumption*
rule.** `read_record` shape-checks only `specHash` (`spec-gate.py:262-279`, specifically
`:277`), so an unknown key passes through as a dict entry - true for pass-through, and
insufficient for consumption: the drift logic would otherwise read a field nothing has
type-checked. The Python side MUST apply the same predicate as `isValidRecord` above and
treat a present-but-not-a-list-of-strings value as a **missing witness** (FR-8.1), never as
an empty or coerced set. The two twins MUST agree on which values are malformed; today the
TypeScript addition drops such a record entirely while Python would coerce it, so the same
file would read `unapproved` in one and `approved`-with-empty-freeze in the other.

The new drift logic sits between `rec = resolve_record(...)` (`:472`) and the phase-band
`continue` (`:501`), per FR-7, and above the `seen_ids` dedup at `:505-507`, which does not
move.

## Invariants

- **INV-1 (no silent gate; constitution invariant 2).** No load-bearing signal added here
  may be written with a swallowed error, and a missing or errored witness fails closed and
  visibly. FR-8 is the direct application, in all three clauses. The migration's pre-flight
  (FR-9.4) is a gate, not a report.
- **INV-2 (approval is a human act).** No agent may mint or restore an approval. A re-stamp
  is a basis change, not a sign-off: `migrated` stays untouched (FR-9.5) and a quarantined
  record stays non-approving until a human acts. FR-9.10's seeding is licensed **only** by
  old-basis freshness, which is a proof about bytes the human already signed - never a
  judgement about what they meant.
- **INV-3 (no status may improve across the basis change).** No sidecar may go from
  `stale`/`unapproved` to `approved` as a consequence of the strip. Stated as a
  **migration-time pre-flight**, deliberately separate from any standing corpus check -
  once the migration writes new-basis hashes, a naive standing version fails on every
  re-stamped record by construction, which is also why FR-9.4 exempts `rehashedAt` records
  from its own second run.
- **INV-4 (the approved set is reconstructible).** After this change, for any approved
  record it must be possible to state what the signature covered without consulting git
  history. FR-1 exists because history stops being able to answer once the strip lands;
  FR-9.10 exists because the property is false for all 61 records until they are seeded.
- **INV-5 (twin parity survives).** `packages/shared/src/canonical.ts` and
  `scripts/hooks/canonical.py` must agree on every input the corpus contains, the embedded
  `CANONICAL_PY` must equal its source, and (new, FR-5) the TypeScript owned-set function
  must agree with `owned_file_set` on the whole corpus. Parity work must run inside a
  harness CI already executes: `scripts/hooks/test_canonical.py` is **not** one - verified,
  `grep -rn test_canonical .github/ package.json .githooks/` returns nothing, and vitest's
  include glob `packages/*/tests/**/*.test.ts` cannot reach a `.py` file (#1669).
- **INV-6 (blast radius; constitution invariant 3).** Nothing here changes behaviour in a
  repo, org or machine-wide config that did not opt in via `.minspec/`.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **AC-1 (FR-1).** A test or check fails on any merged state where the widened strip is
  present in `canonical.ts` while `approveSpec` does not write `ownedAtApproval`, or the
  gate does not read it, **or any approved sidecar in the corpus still carries an old-basis
  hash**. Asserted by executing all three halves, not by reading source text. The merged
  state must contain zero newly-stale approved sidecars.
- **AC-2 (FR-2).** For a fixture carrying all five keys in both inline and block form, the
  TypeScript hash, the Python hash, and the hash produced by the decoded `CANONICAL_PY`
  agree, and all three differ from the pre-change hash exactly on the ownership keys. A
  control asserting `status:` and `phases:` behaviour is unchanged runs in the same suite.
- **AC-3 (FR-2, the branch trap).** A fixture with `implements:` as a bare key over
  indented children leaves **no orphan child line** in the canonical form. Red against a
  `status:`-branch implementation, green against a `phases:`-branch one.
- **AC-4 (FR-3).** A spec declaring seven paths in block form either resolves all seven or
  is rejected with an actionable error. Never resolves one.
- **AC-5 (FR-4, round-trip).** `writeRecord` then `readRecord` preserves a **non-empty**
  `ownedAtApproval`. The `rec()` fixture at `packages/minspec/tests/approval-store.test.ts:25-34`
  must be extended, and the assertion must not use a value that is `undefined` on both sides
  - `toEqual` ignores `undefined`-valued keys, so a lazily-defaulted fixture passes
  vacuously. Tests are outside every tsconfig `include` (`packages/minspec/tsconfig.json`
  is `["src/**/*.ts"]`), so `tsc` will never point at the omission.
- **AC-6 (FR-4, producer - the value, not the key).** Running the real `approveSpec` against
  a temp workspace whose spec declares **at least two real owned paths plus one token the
  gate rejects** (a bareword and a wrong-extension path) produces a sidecar whose written
  `ownedAtApproval` **equals the FR-5 function's output for that spec** - exact set, not key
  presence. Key-presence alone is insufficient: a producer writing a constant `[]`, the raw
  untokenized `implements:` strings, or a set resolved before the FR-3 parser fix would
  satisfy AC-5 (a store-level round-trip on a synthetic fixture) and AC-7 (which exercises
  the function, never `approveSpec`), which is the `reviewStart` failure one level up. The
  set assertion is additionally made on the **raw file text**, following the pattern at
  `approval-store.test.ts:94-99` and explicitly not the pattern at
  `approve-baseline.test.ts:467-476`, which compares `readBack?.field` to `record.field` and
  is green when both are absent.
- **AC-7 (FR-5).** The TypeScript owned-set function and the Python `owned_file_set` return
  the same set for every T3/T4 spec in the corpus, plus fixtures covering: inline comment on
  an ownership line, `implements: none`, a non-existent declared path, a `../` escape, an
  `_INFRA_PREFIXES` path, a non-source extension, and the fuzzy `tasks.md` backtick arm. A
  **separate** assertion pins that the function also exposes the **structured-only** set and
  that the two differ on `specs/minspec/SPEC-017-trust-dashboard/requirements.md` (structured
  empty, full set 22).
- **AC-8 (FR-6, removal direction - the assertion whose absence let the first design ship
  an empty freeze).** Given an approved spec with `ownedAtApproval: ["packages/x/src/a.ts"]`,
  rewriting its frontmatter to `implements: none` MUST cause the gate to **DENY** a write to

## Open Questions

**OQ-1 - One gate verdict or two?** DR-088's "Still open" item 1. `ownership-drifted` (FR-6)
and `ownership.witness.missing` (FR-8) share the union rule and differ only in whether a
well-formed snapshot exists. *Recommendation: two verdicts, one code path.* **Cost:** two
strings to keep consistent in the deny reason and in any dashboard that groups them, and a
small risk they drift apart in wording. The reason to pay it is that the two mean different
things to the human - "you changed the declaration" is a decision to ack, "this approval has
no witness" is a defect the FR-8.3 corpus gate should already have refused - and collapsing
them hides that second, rarer case behind the common one. Note that FR-9.10 makes the
witness-missing population **zero** post-migration, so the second verdict is a backstop, not
a day-one workflow.

**OQ-2 - What do adopters get, given the spec-gate is not scaffolded?** DR-088's "Still
open" item 2, re-posed because its premise was wrong: there is no scaffolded gate to attach
a flag to (FR-11). What adopters receive is the widened `canonical.py` underneath
`scripts/approval-provenance.py`, a merge-gating review input, with no snapshot, no gate and
no migration. Three real options:
(a) ship an adopter-side migration alongside the template refresh;
(b) gate the strip behind a hasher-version or `.minspec/config.json` flag, so an adopter's
provenance tool keeps computing the old basis until they opt in;
(c) accept the break and document it.
*Recommendation: (b), a hasher-version flag.* **Cost:** a versioned hasher is a second basis
that must be kept alive and tested in both twins for as long as any adopter has not migrated,
which is exactly the "two canonical forms" state DR-012 exists to avoid; and a flag defaulting
to "old" means the strip does not reach adopters at all until someone flips it, so #1649's
equivalent stays blocked in their repos. Option (a) is cleaner but ships a corpus-rewriting
script into repos MinSpec cannot test against, and (c) puts a `MISMATCH - a real finding`
verdict in front of adopters' reviewers on valid records, which teaches them to distrust the
one block that is never supposed to be noise.

**OQ-3 - Fix the block-form parser here, or mandate inline form and gate on it?** FR-3.
Option (a) fixes `fm_value`/`rawFrontmatterField` in both twins; option (b) rejects block
form at write time. *Recommendation: (a), fix the parser, in this spec's first slice and
before any snapshot is written.* **Cost:** it widens an already-large T4 change with a
parser fix that moves 13 corpus declarations from partially-owned to fully-owned, which
will freeze files that were quietly editable - #1961 measures 38 paths across 11 specs, so
the day the fix lands the gate's blocking set grows by roughly that much, and any
unapproved spec among the 11 becomes a real blocker for whoever is editing those files.
Option (b) is cheaper and defers the truncation, but it leaves a signed record whose
contents were produced by a known-broken parser, **and it is incompatible with OQ-4's
recommended ordering**: SPEC-058, 059, 060 and 061 all use block-form `affects:` and all
four need a post-migration re-approval under FR-10, which option (b) would refuse until
someone rewrites their declarations to inline form. Choosing (b) therefore also chooses that
rewrite; the Plan must not leave both open.

**OQ-4 - Who pays for the prose correction, and in what order?** FR-10. Correcting a
frontmatter comment or body prose in an approved spec stales that approval, and the
re-derived union is **23 files, 19 approved**. Three orderings: (i) correct first, then
migrate - the corrections go stale under the old basis and FR-9.3 refuses them, so each
needs a human re-approval before the migration runs; (ii) migrate first, then correct - each
correction stales a freshly re-stamped record and needs a human re-approval after; (iii)
correct in the same commit as the migration and let the re-stamp cover it - **rejected**, it
is laundering: the human never signed the corrected text. *Recommendation: (ii), migrate
first, then correct, and correct only the claims the change makes literally FALSE* - the
**10 approved files** that state the two-key strip set (7 body-prose files: SPEC-022,
SPEC-035, SPEC-038, SPEC-051, SPEC-058, SPEC-059, SPEC-061; plus 4 approved frontmatter
newer-variant files: SPEC-050, SPEC-051, SPEC-052, SPEC-060), together with
`hashLockReminder`'s output for newly created specs - rather than all 19. **Cost:** the
older *"ANY edit voids approval"* wording survives in **11 approved files** until each is
next re-approved for its own reasons, and that wording is already wrong today and becomes
more wrong; in a product whose differentiator is that the signpost is never wrong, a
knowingly-retained false statement is the most expensive kind of debt. A human must confirm
they accept that, because the alternative is 19 re-reads. Note the interaction with OQ-3
option (b) above.

**OQ-5 - Does ownership drift freeze an ARCHIVED spec?** `phase_intent_status` returns
`archived` for an explicitly archived spec (`spec-gate.py:221-222`), and the band drops it
today. Moving the evaluation above the band (FR-7) makes archived specs drift-evaluable
unless they are excluded explicitly. Zero T3/T4 specs are archived at `719878d6`, so there
is no blast radius yet. *Recommendation: exclude archived specs explicitly, at the drift
check, with a comment saying why.* **Cost:** archiving becomes a way to release a frozen set
without a human ack - a small, deliberate hole that should be noted in the deny reason so it
is discoverable rather than discovered.

**OQ-6 - What happens to the three sidecars no approve command can reach?**
`specs/minspec/design.md`, `specs/minspec/tasks.md` (both stale today) and
`specs/agent-execute/SPEC-019-execution-substrate/design.md` key paths that
`classifyApprovablePath` no longer treats as approvables (`approvable.ts:26,:36-42`), and
`MinSpec: Approve Spec` enumerates via `listSpecs`, which cannot see them. Quarantining them
creates a permanent non-approving state with no human exit. *Recommendation: retire the
three records as part of this migration (delete the sidecar, record the retirement in the
run report), rather than quarantining them.* **Cost:** it destroys `approvedBy`/`approvedAt`
provenance for three genuine historical sign-offs, which FR-9.4 forbids for quarantine
precisely because provenance is worth keeping - so this is an explicit, human-authorized
exception, not a rule.

**OQ-7 - Does `ownedAtApproval` record which key a path came from?** The gate merges
`implements` and `affects` into one indistinguishable flat set (`spec-gate.py:362-364`), so
a snapshot of the merged set cannot later answer the question, and the provenance is
unrecoverable. *Recommendation: snapshot the merged structured set only, matching the gate's
own merge exactly.* **Cost:** a future requirement that treats the two keys differently (the
validator already does - `declaresCode` counts `implements` alone at `spec-validator.ts:806`)
cannot be answered from historical records and would need a second corpus migration.

**OQ-8 - What is the batched ack, concretely?** DR-088 describes the grandfathered set
taking "one batched ack" as the human act that records "yes, this is the ownership I mean",
but does not say what the human touches. Under FR-9.10 the day-one population is **zero**,
so this is no longer a launch blocker; it becomes the affordance the #1649 backfill needs.
*Recommendation: a single `MinSpec: Acknowledge Ownership Drift` palette command over a
reviewed list, writing `ownedAtApproval` for the selected records in one write, with a
keyboard path on the list.* **Cost:** a bulk affordance over an approval-adjacent record is
exactly the shape that makes bulk-approve dangerous, and the mitigation (the human reads the
list) is the mitigation that bulk actions always erode. A per-spec ack is safer and will not
get done for a 20-spec backfill.

**OQ-9 - Does the sidecar also record `implements_reason`?** FR-6's inert cohort. 13 of the
34 in-band T3/T4 spec files declare `implements: none`, for which both owned sets are
permanently empty and drift can never fire, while FR-2 removes the escape's justification
from the hash and `spec-validator.ts:807` makes that justification load-bearing.
*Recommendation: record `implementsReasonAtApproval?: string` alongside `ownedAtApproval`
and treat a change to it as drift for that cohort.* **Cost:** a second snapshotted field

accept it - leaves a documented hole in 13 of 34 in-band specs, which is why it is a
question and not a default. Re-litigating whether `implements_reason` is stripped at all is
**out of scope**: DR-088 decided it on its own argument.
