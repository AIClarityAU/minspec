# DR-072: The human-approval exit from a triage hold

> **Born `proposed` per DR-029** — acceptance is a separate human act
> (*MinSpec: Accept ADR*). The mechanism ships with the DR because the gate is
> otherwise a one-way door; what acceptance settles is the **policy**, in §2 and §3.

## Context

### The regression this repairs is my own

**#983** closed a real hole: `dispatch-ready-check.sh` re-validated only *state*
(closed / label removed / countermanding label added) and never asked whether an
**affirming verdict existed and still held**. So any writer of the `agent-ready`
label — a human clicking it, a bulk `gh issue edit`, any script — inherited the triage
gate's authority without ever passing through it. Five hand-flipped issues
(#118/#299/#326/#357/#440) dispatched in production and burned build-agent tokens.
The fix made dispatch demand a machine-readable verdict record, `bodyHash`-bound.

It also, unnoticed, made `needs-review` a **one-way door**. The only writer of such a
record was the LLM triage gate, and re-running it re-derives the same hold. A human who
had read an issue and wanted to say *"I've reviewed this, go"* had no way to say it.

That is not a conservative gate; it is a **broken** one. #983's own contract said *fail
toward HOLD, never toward falsely aborting valid work* — "a gate that refuses valid work
is worse than the hole it closed". This DR records how the exit is built so that it does
not simply reopen #983 wearing a friendlier name.

### Why this needs a DR at all

Three properties push it past the "undoable in a day" line:

1. **It widens who may authorise dispatch.** That is a governance change, not a code change.
2. **It creates a corpus-forming record schema.** DR-070's own *Costly to Refactor* section
   makes the point: once N issues carry a machine-readable block, changing its vocabulary
   means a backfill across every open issue and every consumer.
3. **It ships with a named, unclosed trust limit** (§4). A control whose weakness is known
   must have that weakness recorded where the next reader will find it, not only in a
   script header.

### The apparent conflict with DR-070 §3, stated plainly

DR-070 §3 says:

> `triage-decide.sh` stays the **sole writer** of `agent-ready`. … There is no second
> admission lane and no path that writes `agent-ready` around the gate.

`scripts/approve-issue.sh` writes `agent-ready` and is not `triage-decide.sh`. Read
literally, this DR breaks that rule, and the PR #1104 architect reviewer was right to
block on it rather than accept a script comment as the reconciliation.

## Decision

### 1. What DR-070 §3's "sole writer" rule actually protects — and its amendment

DR-070 §3 sits inside a DR about a **standing, unattended policy**: a predicate that
promotes issues with no human in the loop. Its whole subject is machine promotion, and
its §5 is titled *"What can NEVER **auto**-dispatch"*. The rule it states is therefore:

> **No automatic path may write `agent-ready` without re-deriving the verdict.**

That is what "sole writer" was defending, and this DR does not weaken it by a byte. A
**named human, having read one specific issue**, is a different actor from a predicate —
it is the actor every gate in this repo exists to defer to. Conflating the two would make
the pre-dispatch gate stricter than the post-build gate, where a human merge keystroke is
the *designed* terminal authority (DR-063).

**Amendment to DR-070 §3** (to be applied on acceptance of this DR): the sole-writer rule
binds **automatic** promotion. A human-authored, attributed, body-bound approval is a
second *authorising party*, never a second *unchecked lane* — it is subject to §2 and §3
below and to every existing conjunct in `dispatch-ready-check.sh`.

### 2. The mechanism — a second record schema, not a second gate

`scripts/approve-issue.sh <N> [--reason "…"]` mints `minspec-human-approval/1`, read by
**the same** `dispatch-ready-check.sh` that reads a triage verdict, on the same terms
plus two conjuncts:

| conjunct | rule |
|---|---|
| `approvedBy` | present, and **not** a bot identity — refused at the writer *and* the reader |
| `supersedes` | names a hold a human may lift (today: `tier`, and nothing else) |
| everything else | unchanged: `bodyHash` freshness, `human_only: no`, `hold: none`, `decision: agent-ready`, no countermanding label |

Five properties make it an exit through the gate rather than around it:

- **No `--force` and no label flip.** There is no override that skips the record. An
  override that skipped it would be #983 verbatim.
- **A fresh triage verdict is a PRECONDITION.** Approving an un-triaged issue is refused,
  so this cannot become an admission lane that skips triage. The human lifts a hold the
  gate actually produced; they do not manufacture permission from nothing.
- **`bodyHash`-bound, exactly like a triage verdict.** Edit the issue after approving and
  the approval re-stales. This reuses DR-062's content-bound staleness rather than
  inventing a second notion of freshness.
- **Last record wins, both directions.** A re-triage after an approval supersedes it; an
  approval after a held triage verdict supersedes that. Both are pinned by test.
- **The policy lives in the reader.** `--may-approve`, `--render-approval` and
  `--is-bot-identity` are pure entry points on `dispatch-ready-check.sh`, so the
  credentialed writer holds no rule of its own to drift out of step with the gate. Same
  reasoning that put the record *grammar* in the reader in #983.

**Deliberately NOT a `--backfill`.** The 305 open `needs-review` issues predate #983 and
carry no record (0 of 30 sampled), so each needs one `scripts/triage-inbox.sh <N>` before
it can be approved. Minting a verdict that no gate produced is the hole itself; re-triage
is also strictly better, since it re-derives against the body as it stands **now**.

### 3. Which holds a human may lift — and which are absolute

| hold | liftable | why |
|---|---|---|
| `tier` | ✅ | "T3/T4 — too much ceremony to auto-build". Human review is the **designed** remedy; lifting it is the act `needs-review` was always asking for. |
| `human` | ❌ | `human_only` is a **content class** (marketing, positioning, copy, legal, `decide`). It governs who may **author** the work, not who may permit it — and **no keystroke transfers authorship**. |
| `info` | ❌ | Triage could not size the issue and asked for information. Approval does not supply it. |
| `unknown` | ❌ | The gate reached no conclusion, so there is nothing to approve. Re-triage. |

`human_only` is asserted **first and independently** of `hold`, mirroring the reader, so a
garbled or forged record cannot reach the affirmative path through a sibling field.

**A mis-CLASSIFIED issue is cured by fixing the gate's INPUT, never by overriding its
output.** Make the issue body unambiguous about its type and re-triage — the `bodyHash`
changes, so that is a genuine re-verdict rather than a rubber stamp. This matters: #898
(*"migrate ~6 approved-pre-implement specs to `status: planning`"*) is a mechanical
frontmatter chore that triage classified `human_only: yes`, plausibly only because it
touches the governance corpus. The remedy is to correct the classification, not to grant a
power to override it — and "reword until the model agrees" is itself an unsatisfactory
answer, which is why a first-class **dispute** path is filed as #1106.

### 4. Provenance — three controls, and the limit they do not cross

1. **Interactive only.** Refuses without a TTY on stdin *and* stdout. No `--yes`, no
   `--force`, deliberately.
2. **Typed confirmation of the issue number**, not `y`.
3. **Identity from the API** (`gh api user`), never a flag; a bot login is refused at both
   the writer and the reader, so the autonomous pipeline can never mint the human keystroke
   it exists to wait for.

**The limit, recorded rather than papered over:** in a container whose `gh` is
authenticated as the maintainer, controls 1 and 2 are the only things separating *"the
human approved this"* from *"something running as the human approved this"*. They raise a
one-command bypass to deliberate forgery — **the same bar #983 settled for with
`bodyHash`** — and no further. Binding a record to its **comment author** is the remaining
hardening; it needs the App-token path and is filed as **#1105** (family of #397).

### 5. A record is only as good as its AUTHOR — the trust boundary this rests on

Added 2026-07-31, while building the label-flip front end (#1113). It belongs in this DR
because §2's whole argument — that an approval is safe *because it is bound to a verdict
the gate produced* — is worthless if anyone can write a verdict.

**The premise §2 inherited from #983 was false.** #983's header reasoned:

> Anyone with **write access** could hand-craft a record comment; what they can no longer
> do is inherit the gate by clicking a label, because a forged record must also carry a
> correct sha256 of the exact triaged body.

`AIClarityAU/minspec` is a **PUBLIC** repository. Both halves of that sentence fail here:

- posting a comment containing a forged record requires **no permission at all**; and
- the `bodyHash` is not the obstacle it was taken for — the issue body is public, so the
  sha256 is computable by anyone. **A hash binds content, never authorship.** It was doing
  real work against *staleness* and none whatever against *forgery*, and the two were
  conflated.

The only permission left standing was the `agent-ready` label — which §2's own front ends
turn into a one-click gesture. A stranger's forged `hold: tier` block plus a maintainer's
ordinary label flip would approve an issue the gate had held.

**Decision: trust records by AUTHOR, which a comment body cannot alter about itself.**
`dispatch-ready-check.sh --trusted-comment-bodies` filters comments *before* any record is
parsed, and every reader goes through it. Trusted =

- this gate's own App login (`minspec-sdd`) — **by login, not by association**, because an
  App reads as `CONTRIBUTOR`; filtering on association alone would reject the very writer
  every record comes from and the gate would refuse everything; **or**
- `authorAssociation` in `OWNER` / `MEMBER` / `COLLABORATOR`.

Two boundaries deliberately *not* widened:

- **Not "any bot".** Matching `*[bot]` would trust `github-actions[bot]`, `dependabot[bot]`
  and every App ever installed on the repo to author verdict records — a worse boundary
  than the one being fixed. Only this gate's App may.
- **Not "any commenter with a plausible record".** Unparseable input emits nothing rather
  than passing the raw text through, so a parse failure fails closed to `no-verdict`.

**An API-shape dependency worth recording, because it fails silently.** The App's login is
`minspec-sdd` via GraphQL (`gh issue view --json comments`) and `minspec-sdd[bot]` via REST
(`gh api .../issues/N/comments`) — measured, not assumed. Every reader uses GraphQL today,
but nothing at the call site says so, and switching one call to REST would silently drop
every bot-authored record and refuse all dispatch with no error explaining why. The
comparison therefore normalises both sides (lowercased, trailing `[bot]` stripped) and
accepts either spelling. **A trust check whose failure mode is "silently refuse everything"
must not depend on an invisible detail of how the data was fetched.**

### 5a. Comment authorship is not RECORD authorship — and the corollary for recency

The author filter above was written with the sentence *"trust is by author, which a comment
body cannot alter about itself"*. True of the **comment**. **False of the record** — and
adversarial review of the diff reproduced why, with no attacker involved:

> The pipeline's own trusted writers **republish text they did not author.** A maintainer
> writing *"the first triage said: ‹pastes record› but I re-ran it"* puts a stale record
> last. `dispatch-issue.sh` posts a build agent's `.agent-summary.md` **verbatim** under a
> trusted identity, and that agent's prompt embeds the untrusted issue body.

Selecting the **textually last** record therefore let stale text win. Demonstrated against
a live `hold: tier` verdict, and against a live `hold: human` / `human_only: yes` verdict —
i.e. an ordinary quote did what §3 declares no approval may ever do.

**Decision: recency is read from the record's own `verdictAt`, never inferred from
position.** A quoted record is always older than the verdict it sits beside, so it cannot
win. Ties (same second) fall back to position, preserving the re-triage-supersedes rule.
One shared selector (`--newest-record`) serves all three readers, because each front end
carrying its own "take the last one" is exactly how two of three would have kept the defect
after the third was fixed.

That makes `verdictAt` load-bearing, so it must now be present, ISO-8601, and **not
future-dated** — the last of which closes the residual where the quoted text is *fabricated*
rather than copied. The clock bound is best-effort by design: if `date` cannot produce one,
that single check is skipped rather than refusing all work, because the primary control
needs no clock and a gate that halts the pipeline over a missing date utility would be a
worse failure than the one it guards.

**The general lesson, worth more than the fix:** *provenance of the container is not
provenance of the contents.* An author check answers "who wrote this comment", never "who
wrote this text" — the moment any trusted party quotes, echoes, or summarises, the two come
apart. Any future signal read out of free-form text needs to ask which of the two it
actually needs.

**What this does NOT close, stated plainly:** a `COLLABORATOR`-authored *triage* record is
still accepted, so the trust boundary is "people with write access" rather than "the gate
itself". Narrowing triage records to bot-authored-only is tracked in #1105, and carries a
sequencing trap: `triage-inbox.sh` posts via bare `gh`, so enforcing it invalidates every
existing record until that script mints its own App token. Nobody has yet audited the
existing corpus for a forged record either.

## Consequences

- **The pre-dispatch gate stops being a trap.** Before this, a legitimately reviewed issue
  was permanently undispatchable. That is the defect being repaired; everything else here
  is the cost of repairing it safely.
- **The yield is per-issue, not bulk.** Each approval costs a re-triage plus a human read.
  This is not a backlog fix and must never be sold as one — see DR-070's Consequences,
  which say the same about its own predicate.
- **`minspec-human-approval/1` becomes a corpus.** Changing its vocabulary later means a
  backfill. The schema is deliberately minimal for that reason, and `RECORD_SCHEMA_HUMAN`
  and `APPROVABLE_HOLDS` are named constants in a tested file so widening the liftable set
  is a visible one-line diff and never an emergent side effect.
- **Two schemas now satisfy the same reader**, so "the gate passed it" is no longer a
  single fact. The reader distinguishes them, and the dispatch audit trail says which
  party authorised the build. A reader that could not tell them apart would report the same
  sentence for a machine verdict and a human one.
- **The merge keystroke is conserved** (DR-070 §4): approval moves the gate, it does not
  remove it. The resulting PR still lands at `awaiting-approval` (DR-063) for a human merge.
- **DR-070 §5's absolutes are untouched.** No configuration value, and now no human
  approval either, reaches `hold:human` / `info` / `unknown`.

## Alternatives considered

- **A `--force` flag on `dispatch-issue.sh` — rejected outright.** An override that skips
  the record is #983 by another name; #1084 says so in its own ask.
- **A `human-approved` LABEL that triage honours — rejected.** A label is precisely the
  click-inheritable signal #983 proved untrustworthy, and it carries neither an approver
  nor a body binding. It would re-create the hole with better manners.
- **Let a human lift `hold:human` with a second, louder flag — rejected.** It erodes a
  standing repo rule (marketing / positioning / legal / copywriting is human-only, never
  agent-landed) via a per-issue click, and the case it is reaching for — *mis*-classification
  — is better served by correcting the classifier's input (#1106).
- **A `--backfill` that mints records for the pre-#983 backlog — rejected.** Minting a
  verdict no gate produced is the hole. Re-triage is the only backfill, as #983 settled.
- **Text-interleaved comment-author markers to close §4's limit now — rejected for v1.**
  Comment bodies are attacker-writable, so a naive `<!-- author: X -->` marker is forgeable
  by any commenter. The two shapes that work (author-filter-before-join, or a
  nonce-delimited envelope) are a separate, tested change — #1105.
- **Do nothing; body-edit until triage relents — rejected as the *only* path.** It works
  and stays the right primary path when the classification is genuinely wrong, but it is
  undocumented, teaches prompt-gaming a safety gate, and **destroys the evidence** that
  triage erred — the one corpus that could measure the classifier's accuracy.

## Costly to Refactor

**Partly.** The script and the liftable-hold set are cheap and reversible — deleting
`approve-issue.sh` and the schema branch restores today's behaviour in one commit, and no
approval can outlive an edit to its issue.

What is not cheap is the same pair DR-070 names: **the record schema becomes a corpus**,
and **what a human approval is taken to mean is a governance premise, not a code path**.
If it turns out that tier-held issues routinely contain decisions a human's "go" did not
actually settle, the correct response is to **narrow `APPROVABLE_HOLDS` to empty and
withdraw this DR**, not to tune the controls.
