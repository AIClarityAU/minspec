## In plain terms - what this changes for you

The constitution gets two lines above everything else. First: make your job smaller.
Second: spend as few tokens as possible, counting the tokens spent fixing mistakes later.
Every other rule has to serve one of those two, and a rule that serves neither gets
removed. When the two pull in different directions, saving your time wins, unless that
costs more than 2% of a week's budget for one decision.

## Context

On 2026-10-17 the founder asked for a review of the constitution, rules, memory,
principles and decision records against two priorities, in this order (queue item in2):

> mimizing human involvement first, then token use second. to minimize token use we need
> to be efficient but also minimise rework [...] the previous project's 3 days of fixing
> for 1 day of functionality was clearly under-spec'd, but i'm open to the possibility
> that minspec is over-spec'd. [...] all other rules/principles, etc cascade out of these
> two core tenets: min human, min tokens

The core-tenets review (2026-10-17, run outside this repository) found the corpus leaning
toward over-specification. The figures, and where each can be checked:

- 104 decision records on `main` by 2026-10-17 (count of `docs/decisions/DR-*.md`), the
  first dated 2026-05-26, about five a week. Each is a founder acceptance under DR-086 §2.2
  (the approval-or-acceptance stop class).
- DR-070 (standing dispatch policy) records 287 open issues held at `needs-review`,
  verified live on 2026-07-26 (DR-070, line 36).
- A repeat-fix share of 6.7% (11 of 165 fix commits over 60 days), far from 333Method's
  3:1. This is the review's figure, computed from fix-commit history by a method that lives
  outside this repository. It is not recomputed here and should be read as unverified.

The constitution had no rule above its invariants that says what the goals are *for*.
G-1 (correctness) ranked above G-3 (just enough human), so a tie between "add a check" and
"spare the founder" resolved toward the check.

## Decision

### 1. A `## Core tenets` section above the invariants

Two ranked tenets:

1. **Make the founder's job smaller - minimise human involvement.** A human gate must name
   the error it catches that no machine check catches, and is retired once it is
   rubber-stamped.
2. **Minimise tokens, counting rework.** Spend on specs, gates and review only where that
   is cheaper than the expected fix. Measure both sides and re-tune.

A rule serving neither is removed. The invariants are not traded against either tenet;
they are the safety bounds both operate inside.

### 2. Goal re-rank

G-3 (just enough human) moves to rank 1. G-1 and G-2 follow as the means of tenet 2
(correctness and no tech debt are rework not paid later). G-4 onwards keep their ranks.
IDs are stable, so every `goal: G-N` reference still resolves. The next-task resolver
reads rank from the list number (`packages/minspec/src/lib/artifact-graph.ts:150`), so
this re-rank changes its tie-break order. That is intended.

### 3. Tie-break: 2% of the weekly budget per decision (change 7)

When the tenets conflict, tenet 1 wins unless honouring it costs more than 2% of the
weekly budget for that one decision. Then tenet 2 wins and the decision record says so,
naming the base used: the share of the weekly quota cap, the same base every token figure
put to the founder must name. The 2% is a starting value, not a measurement.

### 4. When this takes effect

The tenets and the goal re-rank are operative text: the next-task resolver reads the
re-rank as soon as it is on `main`. So they take effect through **the founder's own merge
keystroke** on the pull request that carries them, and not through anything an agent
does. `.minspec/constitution.md` and `CLAUDE.md` are boundary paths
(`scripts/auto-merge-gate.ts:597`), so auto-merge cannot land them. That keystroke is the
ratification of the operative text, the same shape DR-086 Amendment A used for the
autonomy setting. Accepting this record (*MinSpec: Accept ADR*) is his separate act of
recording the rationale. If he wants neither in force yet, he does not merge.

## Rejected alternatives (DR-086 §4)

- **Tenets as a principle, not above the invariants.** Rejected. Principles "can be bent
  with justification", so a cascade root that can be bent is not a root.
- **Rank tokens first.** Rejected. The founder ranked them in that order in his own words.
- **No numeric tie-break ("use judgement").** Rejected. That is an unenumerable,
  self-judged rule, the shape DR-086 §2 refuses for "seriously stuck".
- **A fixed token count instead of a share of budget.** Rejected. The quota changes and
  models change price. A share stays meaningful across both.
- **Promote G-3 by rewriting G-1/G-2 away.** Rejected. Correctness and no-debt still earn
  their place, as tenet 2's means. Removing them would invite the 333Method failure back.

## Consequences

- Every later goal-rank tie-break shifts toward sparing founder attention.
- Every rule can now be audited by asking which tenet it serves. Rules that serve neither
  are candidates for removal (core-tenets-review-1017 lists the first batch).
- The global `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` should carry the same block. That file is outside
  this repo, so this record cannot change it (constitution invariant 3). The text is
  staged as a patch for the founder to apply on the host.
- **Accepted risk:** the 2% threshold is a guess. A too-high value lets founder-saving
  choices burn tokens; a too-low value hands decisions back to token frugality.
