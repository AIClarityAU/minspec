# Delegated approvals land through a lane of their own

> **Born `proposed` per DR-029.** Acceptance is a separate human act (*MinSpec: Accept
> ADR*). This record is `kind: direction`, which no delegation covers, and an agent never
> mints the sign-off. Nothing in it takes effect before the founder accepts it: the lane
> reads this file's `status:` on `main` on every run and merges nothing unless it reads
> `accepted`.

> **On this record's number.** It is one above the highest id on `main` and in open pull
> requests, read 2026-10-30 at `f686cb25`. The collision gate re-checks it on this record's
> own pull request. Bodies here do not spell local ids of 100 and above, per the
> parent-register reference gate (#2148), so records in that range are named by subject.

## In plain terms - what this changes for you

The bot already approves specs under its own name, on your delegation, after an AI reviewer
passed them. Those approval pull requests then wait for your merge, because every rule that
lets the bot merge was written before this case existed. With this decision, a check running
from `main` merges one when it proves the pull request carries that one approval and nothing
else, that the approved text is byte-for-byte what the reviewer passed, and that CI is green
on the exact commit. It works through them one at a time, because each merge puts the rest
behind `main`. No model runs per approval, and you do nothing after accepting: no install, no
ruleset command, no workflow push. The cost you accept: a wrong approval that the AI reviewer
missed reaches `main` with no human look, and the second opinion it rests on is the same
review, re-verified by a machine and not repeated by another model. Spec-writing stays paused
after this lands (see "What this does not fix").

## Context

The bot records delegated spec approvals, and no rule lets anything but the founder merge them, so they queue on a keystroke the delegation was meant to remove.

**What blocks them.** The caller that opens these pull requests never merges
(`scripts/lib/delegated-approval-caller.ts:10`). The approval-only lane admits founder-authored
records only (`scripts/lib/alta-approval-only.ts:298,260`). The delegated spec-merge arm refuses
any pull request that moves `status:` (`scripts/lib/delegated-merge.ts:197-210`). The runner's
merge verb refuses `.minspec/approvals/` (`KEPT_PREFIX` in `cos-runner-gh.sh`). The three merged
so far (#3033, #3156, #3157) were all merged by the founder.

**Measured 2026-10-30, 08:00 UTC, on `main` at `f686cb25`, read-only.** The 24 open
`dlg-approve/*` pull requests are all `BEHIND` and `MERGEABLE`, each with two changed files.
Running the conditions in section 1 over them by hand:

| Count | Pull requests | Outcome under section 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | #3154, #3155, #3244, #3356, #3358 to #3361, #3363 to #3365, #3370 to #3374 | Eligible once brought up to date |
| 1 | #3357 | Refused: `test` failed on its head. All test files passed and the step exited 1 after the coverage table; the cause is not diagnosed here. #3036 owns red ones |
| 1 | #3362 | Refused: the record's hash is not the head's, because the approval rewrote a body `**Status:**` line. The AI panel refused it too |
| 6 | #3085, #3087 to #3091 | Refused: `hold:human`. Five would be refused without the label: #3087, #3089 and #3091 have Clarify open on `main`, and #3088, #3089 and #3090 approve text that `main` has since changed |

So the conditions catch the known-defective approvals from an earlier audit without any human
looking, and the only one of the 24 that a label alone holds is #3085.

**What the existing verdict actually verifies.** A delegated record cites `verdictBy`, a review
comment on the content pull request. When the record is written, `planDelegatedApproval`
requires that pull request to be merged, to have changed the artifact, and to hold the same
`specHash` at its head as the text being approved. `judgeHeadReviewPass` requires an `ai-review`
check-run from the reviewer App with conclusion success on that head sha, a latest review comment
of `ai-review:pass`, and that comment posted inside a completed CI run on that sha. So the verdict
is bound to a sha, and through the hash to the exact text being approved. Re-run over the 24
above, the verdict and the reviewed bytes held for all 24, including the three whose text `main`
has since changed: those verdicts are true of older text, which item 6 of section 1 refuses. Two
gaps:

- The record's offline check (`checkApprovalActor`) checks only that the URL is a comment on the
  right pull request, not what the comment says, and the App token that could write such a record
  is available to agent sessions. So the lane must re-read the evidence itself.
- `scripts/approval-integrity.ts` is in no workflow on `main` (nothing under `.github/` names
  it), although the subject of `f686cb25` says otherwise: that commit is a 354-line spec and
  nothing else. The lane cannot lean on it, so it calls the same predicate itself.

The approval pull request also gets its own `ai-review` round. A base update needs no new one:
the verdict is carried when the reviewer's input is byte-identical (seen on #3085, posted 28
seconds after the push).

**Why the second review is dropped.** The bot-merge record's second reviewer exists because a
diff of code needs a judge that did not write it. Here the text was judged on its content pull
request, the approval pull request was judged by the panel, and the remaining question is
mechanical: is this the text that was judged? A third model reading the same text answers nothing
the hash does not.

**Strict.** The ruleset requires a branch up to date with `main` (`strict: true`), so each merge
puts the rest behind. Nothing updates these today except by hand: #3085 holds 18 merge commits
from `main`, each a full CI round that led to no merge.

## The choice

**A.** A narrow carve-out in the bot-merge record: the runner merges these through its helper,
each with its own review job on a current head. **B.** A delegated twin of the approval-only
self-merge, running in the repo and working through them one at a time. Measured base, one formula
(`cos.py usage`, billed = input + output + cache writes): the weekly quota cap, where 14% used
equalled 12.2M billed since the window reset, so **1% of the week is about 0.87M billed**. That is
one early reading of a rounded percentage.

| For 24 pull requests | A: runner and helper | **B: lane in the repo (rec)** |
|---|---|---|
| Heads that must pass CI | about 30 (24 / 0.79: 79% of 15-minute windows see no merge to `main`) | about 28 (24 / 0.875: 87.5% of 8-minute windows see none) |
| CI workflow runs, at 8 distinct workflows per head | about 240 | about 224 (public repo, no minutes billed) |
| Elapsed under strict | 8 to 30 hours (one merge per runner run, runs 15 to 60 minutes apart) | about 4 hours (CI spans 5 to 8 minutes per head, measured on 18 of them, so about 10 minutes per merge) |
| Model tokens per approval | 110k to 240k billed: a review job (84k and 216k on two recent jobs, neither this exact job) plus about three runner calls at 8.8k | 0 when the verdict carries |
| Model tokens for the 24 | 2.6M to 5.8M, **3.0% to 6.7% of the week**, and the same again for each later batch | **0** |
| One-off build | about 0.2M, 0.2% (estimate) | 1M to 2M, **1.1% to 2.3%** (estimate; median dispatch was 225k, and this has a judge and mutation tests) |
| Founder steps after accepting | install a `~/.claude` patch | none |

The elapsed figures for B are modelled, since the lane does not exist. B costs more once and
pays back within its first 4 to 18 approvals, and 89 specs are in the pipe.

**Decision: B.** Its primary cost is a second merge path to maintain, whose defect would land
something unreviewed; the same risk the approval-only record accepted, bounded the same way.

## Decision

### 1. When the lane merges

The lane merges a pull request by squash, as `minspec-sdd[bot]`, only when **all** of these hold.
Anything unreadable refuses, and an error turns the run red (constitution invariant 2).

1. **This record is `accepted` on `main`**, read on every run. The repo is AIClarityAU/minspec;
   memory-fabric is refused by a hard-coded name.
2. **The pull request is the bot's.** Open, not a draft, from this repo, targeting the default
   branch; author is user type `Bot` and login `minspec-sdd[bot]`, keyed on type as #3212 requires;
   every non-merge commit is attributed to that account; no merge commit touches a file the pull
   request changes. The branch name proves nothing and is not read as evidence.
3. **Its labels clear it.** `ai-review:pass`; no `ai-review:changes`, `ai-review:blocked` or
   `hold:*`; no shepherd hand-off comment to the founder.
4. **The whole diff is one approval, proven from the objects.** With the head containing the base
   tip, `git diff --raw` holds exactly two entries, both regular files: the record
   `.minspec/approvals/specs/<project>/SPEC-NNN-<slug>/requirements.md.json`, and that spec's
   `requirements.md`, modified. A plan artifact, a decision record, `INDEX.md` or any other path
   refuses, so engineering-record acceptances stay out of this lane.
5. **The spec's text did not move.** With the frontmatter `status:` line and `phases:` block
   removed by `stripLifecycleStrict`, base and head are byte-identical. On the base the status is
   `new` or `specifying`; on the head it is `planning` or `implementing`.
6. **The hash matches the base at merge.** The record's `specHash` equals `specHash()` of the spec
   on the base tip being merged onto, and on the head.
7. **The record is delegated and covered.** Known keys only; `approvedBy` and `actor` are the bot;
   `basis` is `delegation:<id>`; `act` is `spec-approval`; tier matches the spec. It passes
   `checkApprovalActor` against the base's `.minspec/delegations.json`, which runs
   `evaluateDelegation`: repo, act and tier covered, the delegation's record accepted and in date,
   no governance path, repeat-fix share known and within its tripwire (5.2% against 13%, today).
8. **The spec may advance.** `openClarifyDetail` and `specNotApprovable` both pass on the base's
   text, so a spec whose Clarify is open does not land even with a record.
9. **The verdict is read again, not trusted.** `verdictBy` is on the pull request that last changed
   the spec, per git. That pull request is merged, `judgeHeadReviewPass` passes on its head sha, and
   `specHash()` of the spec at that sha equals the record's.
10. **CI is green on the head.** Every latest check on the head has finished SUCCESS, SKIPPED or
    NEUTRAL, and every required check in the ruleset is present and green from its required
    producer. Zero checks, a pending check, a failing check or a missing one refuses. Nothing is
    bypassed, and these pull requests are not machinery (`machinery-review-required` succeeds on
    them), so no self-certification gate is involved.
11. **The merge is pinned.** The base tip is re-read, then the merge API is called with
    `sha=<judged head>`. Never `--admin`, never auto-merge, never a force-push.

### 2. One at a time

A run that finds no lane pull request in flight takes the oldest that meets everything in section
1 except being up to date, and updates it (`PUT .../update-branch`). "In flight" means up to date
with `main` and checks not finished. CI's completion fires the existing `workflow_run` trigger,
which judges and merges it, and that run takes the next. A pull request `main` overtook during CI is
updated again. Two runs racing costs one extra CI round and cannot land anything wrong, since GitHub
refuses a stale merge. A pull request that fails a condition is skipped by name in the run summary,
so it never blocks the rest.

**Update refused.** The App lacks `workflows` scope, so GitHub refuses an update when `main` has
moved a workflow file since the branch's merge base (three such commits in the last 14 days; none
affects the 16 above). The lane closes that pull request with a comment and deletes its branch, so
the caller reopens it on a current base at the cost of one review round. Its branch name is
deterministic and this is the caller's existing path.

### 3. No per-approval model

No second-reviewer job runs for an approval in this lane. Section 1 items 6 and 9 replace it, as the
Context explains. The bot-merge record's condition (b) stands everywhere else.

### 4. Where it lives

In `scripts/alta-self-merge.ts`, which dispatches on the author, and a new judge in `scripts/lib/`.
**No workflow file changes:** `alta-self-merge.yml` already has the triggers, the App token and the
main-only trust boundary, so the App can land it, under the bot-merge record's machinery path. **No
`~/.claude` change:** the runner's helper keeps `KEPT_PREFIX` unchanged and still refuses these, so
there is one merger for them. The sentence in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` that says the bot merges "only
through `cos-runner-gh.sh pr merge`" governs agent sessions, as it already does beside the
approval-only workflow.

### 5. What stays the founder's

memory-fabric; plan artifacts; any decision record; the constitution, `.minspec/delegations.json`,
DR-086 and the bot-merge record, none of which this lane can reach and none of which this record
edits; `--admin`; and any pull request with a `hold:*` label. The approval-only lane
(`scripts/lib/alta-approval-only.ts`, `judgeApprovalOnly`) is unchanged, byte for byte.

## What this does not fix

Landing the 16 takes specs on `main` at `new` or `specifying` from 89 to 73, and the drain's cap is
40, so **spec-writing stays paused** until at least 34 more move. Of the 89: 60 are touched by an
open pull request (these among them), 28 have Clarify open, 1 is not approvable. This lane is
necessary for that, not sufficient.

## Cost

- **The named risk:** a wrong approval lands without the founder (the delegated-approval record's
  accepted risk). What now stands between it and `main` is the AI panel, section 1 and the
  tripwire. #3104 stays a live hole here: a spec with Clarify pending and no decision text detected
  is admitted, and this lane inherits that until #3104 is fixed.
- **A defect in the judge lands something unreviewed.** Bounded by: it runs from `main` only, so no
  pull request can change the check that judges it; a test whose mutant removing each guard goes red;
  the ruleset still decides; the merge is bound to the head.
- **More merges to `main` in a burst.** About six an hour while a backlog drains, against a median
  gap of 18 minutes, so other open pull requests go behind more often for those hours.
- **The App's bypass is untouched and unused.** This lane cannot use `--admin`, but the token it holds
  is the token the bypass is attached to. The bot-merge record's Cost section already names that, and
  #3039 is still open; until it ships no machine audits bot merges.

## Tripwire

The lane refuses every merge and fails its run red when either holds:

1. The delegation's own: the repeat-fix share is above 13% or unknown (`evaluateDelegation`).
2. Two or more lane-merged approvals in the last 30 days were undone, read from `main`'s history: a
   commit that deletes or re-writes a record whose `basis` starts `delegation:`, or a revert of a
   commit titled `chore(approve)`. The starting value is a guess and gets re-tuned on data.

## Rollback

Move this record to `deprecated`: the lane reads its `status:` every run, so it stops at once and the
approval-only lane carries on untouched. A single wrong approval is undone by reverting its squash
commit, which also counts toward the tripwire. A `hold:*` label stops one pull request.

## Rejected alternatives (DR-086 §4)

- **A, the narrow carve-out in the bot-merge record.** Rejected: 3.0% to 6.7% of the week per 24
  approvals forever, up to 30 hours of elapsed time, and a host patch for the founder to install, all
  to have a model re-read text a hash already binds.
- **One pull request carrying many approvals.** The approval-only judge already reads many records,
  and it would need one CI round for all 24. Rejected for now: one bad spec holds every other one, a
  fresh panel round is spent on the whole batch, the 24 would be closed and reopened, and one approval
  could not be reverted alone. Serial throughput of about six an hour exceeds the supply. Reconsider if
  the queue stays over 24 for a week.
- **Merge queue, or turning `strict` off.** Rejected: a ruleset change for one lane, and the approval-only
  record names `strict` as what closes its base window.
- **Arm native auto-merge.** Rejected, as in the approval-only and bot-merge records: it stays armed
  across later pushes.
- **Widen the approval-only judge to admit bot records.** Rejected: its founder guards are the whole
  reason a defect there is bounded, and a shared judge puts that lane at risk. Separate judges share
  the small pure helpers.
- **Keep the founder's keystroke.** Rejected: a human gate must name the error only it catches (core
  tenet 1). The bot-merge record's audit found 200 queued merges and no catch, and 24 are waiting here.
- **Lean on `approval-integrity` in CI.** Rejected: it is not wired into any workflow.

## Consequences

- Once this is accepted and the build has landed, covered spec approvals stop reaching the founder's
  queue, and `awaiting-approval` on them stops meaning him.
- The audit trail shows `minspec-sdd[bot]` as approver and merger, truthfully, with one comment per
  merge naming the record, the head, the base and the verdict.
- The runner gets no new work and no new merge path, and #3084 can close.
