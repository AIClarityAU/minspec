# DR-088: Ownership leaves the hash, and the alarm that replaced it does not yet work

> **Born `proposed` per DR-029.** Acceptance is a separate human act (*MinSpec: Accept
> ADR*); an agent never mints the sign-off. This record's live status is its frontmatter
> `status:` field, not this note.

> **This record is deliberately incomplete, and says where.** §1 and §3 are proposed
> decisions that survived two rounds of adversarial review. §2 is an open design
> question: two independent reviewers, confirmed by measurement here, showed that the
> compensating control drafted for it does not reach the population it exists to protect.
> Recording a control that is measurably inert would be the exact defect this project
> exists to prevent, so it is recorded as unsolved instead.

## In plain terms

Today, adding an `implements:` line to an approved spec voids your signature on it,
because that line is inside the bytes you signed. That is why 24 already-approved specs
carry no ownership declaration: backfilling one would throw away a real sign-off to
record a mechanical fact you never wrote.

The founder decided to take those lines out of the signature, which makes the backfill
free. The open problem is what replaces the alarm they were carrying. The obvious answer
- have the commit-time gate refuse on an ownership change - was measured and does not
work: it cannot see 22 of the 24 specs concerned, and in the one direction that matters
most it refuses nothing at all.

## Context

The founder decided on 2026-08-23, answering
[#1481 (should `implements:` be part of the canonicalized spec hash?)](https://github.com/AIClarityAU/minspec/issues/1481),
that `implements:` comes out of the canonical approval hash. This record does not
relitigate that. It answers what exactly leaves, how the existing approvals migrate, and
- honestly - fails to answer what replaces the lost alarm.

### The mechanism

`canonicalizeSpec` ([canonical.ts:89-123](../../packages/shared/src/canonical.ts#L89-L123))
strips exactly two frontmatter keys, `status:` and `phases:`, via `stripLifecycle`
([canonical.ts:60-83](../../packages/shared/src/canonical.ts#L60-L83)); the Python twin
does the same in `_strip_lifecycle`
([canonical.py:37-53](../../scripts/hooks/canonical.py#L37-L53)). Everything else in
frontmatter is hashed content, so `implements:`, `affects:` and `implements_reason:` are
inside the signed bytes.

Verified with a control rather than inferred: adding an `implements:` line to SPEC-004
changes `specHash`; editing `status:` does not.

```
sidecar    : d8d24d23…6674
current    : d8d24d23…6674   == approval fresh
+implements: 2e4d4b1d…6214   != CHANGED -> approval would stale
status edit: d8d24d23…6674   == unchanged (control: lifecycle key IS stripped)
```

**These numbers move under you.** They were measured three times during this record's
drafting, on three commits, and changed each time as sidecars landed. The migration must
recompute at its own commit and must never trust a number written here.

## Decision

### 1. Three keys leave the canonical hash

`stripLifecycle` gains `implements`, `affects` and `implements_reason` alongside `status`
and `phases`, with the same indented-children rule.

The criterion is **frontmatter keys whose values are owned-code paths, plus the single
field that validates the `implements: none` escape** - not "keys the gate reads", which
was the first draft's wording and does not select this set. The gate also reads `tier`
and `id` ([spec-gate.py:448-450](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L448-L450)), and `tier`
is *more* gate-arming than `affects`: a silent `T4` to `T2` edit drops a spec out of the
gated set entirely at `:450`. Those stay hashed. Reading-a-key is not the criterion;
naming-an-owned-path is.

- **`implements:`** - the decision in #1481, and the key the spec-gate turns into its
  block set.
- **`affects:`** - fed into the same block set by the same loop
  ([spec-gate.py:350-352](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L350-L352) iterates
  `("implements", "affects")`). Leaving it hashed would mean an author still cannot
  record a shared-surface edit without voiding a signature. Validator parity is only
  partial, and the record should not overstate it: `declaresCode` counts `implements`
  tokens alone ([spec-validator.ts:806](../../packages/minspec/src/lib/spec-validator.ts#L806)).
- **`implements_reason:`** - a validator input, not prose: the `none` escape is satisfied
  only when it is present
  ([spec-validator.ts:807](../../packages/minspec/src/lib/spec-validator.ts#L807)).
  Leaving it hashed would let the escape's justification void a signature while the
  escape itself did not.

**One measured hazard for the backfill.** A full-line `#` comment in frontmatter is
hashed content, so an explanatory comment added above `implements:` re-voids the sign-off
the strip just preserved. An *inline* comment on an ownership key line is dropped
wholesale with the line. The #1649 backfill must therefore add **key lines only**.

### 2. UNRESOLVED - what replaces the alarm

This is the half this record does not close, and the reason it is proposed rather than
complete.

**What is being given up is narrower than "the gate stops working".** Hashing
`implements:` never blocked an ownership edit: a spec's own markdown is permanently
unownable (`.md` is not in `_SRC_EXT_RE`,
[spec-gate.py:285-287](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L285-L287)), and an *approved*
spec blocks nothing at all - only `unapproved` and `stale` specs enter the blocking set
([spec-gate.py:500-501](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L500-L501)). What hashing bought
was that **rewriting the ownership claim was loud and self-punishing**: the edit staled
the approval, the spec surfaced under "Needs Re-Approval", and the human re-read before
signing again. After §1, that edit is silent with the human's name still behind it.

**The drafted replacement was: record a second digest over the ownership lines, and have
`ownership.drifted` arm the spec-gate. Measurement refutes it, twice over.**

- **It cannot see the population it is for.** The gate skips any spec whose phase intent
  is not `implementing`/`done`
  ([spec-gate.py:488-489](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L488-L489)), and that `continue`
  executes *before* the owned set is computed at `:498` and before `blocking.append` at
  `:501`. Measured over the 24 approved T3/T4 specs with no `implements:`: **2 are
  reachable, 22 are skipped** (21 have no `phases:` block at all, one is `specifying`).
  The cost estimate that accompanied the recommendation - "the backfill freezes 24 specs'
  files" - is therefore wrong by an order of magnitude; the real figure is 2.
- **It is empty in the direction that matters most.** The block set is derived from the
  spec's *current* declaration
  ([owned_file_set](../../scripts/hooks/spec-gate.py#L365-L376)), whose own docstring
  states the consequence: *"A spec that declares NO impl files owns NOTHING and therefore
  blocks nothing."* So rewriting an approved spec's `implements:` to `none` - the silent
  disarm - produces an **empty** freeze. The control refuses nothing in exactly the case
  it exists to catch.

**The fallback is already rejected on this record's own evidence.** Making drift a
validator error only, without the gate, is the "advisory" shape: `npm run validate`
currently prints `140 non-fatal warning(s)` and then `Frontmatter validation passed.`, and
`.githooks/pre-commit` allows a red corpus it did not introduce. A warning in that stream
is not an alarm.

**Constraints any real control must satisfy** (these are the durable output of the two
review rounds, and the next attempt should start here):

1. Reach specs the gate currently skips, or explicitly re-argue constitution invariant 2
   for the 22 without it.
2. Refuse on ownership **removal**, which means the freeze scope cannot be derived from
   the post-edit bytes - it must draw on the owned set **as it stood at approval** (a
   sibling of [#1642](https://github.com/AIClarityAU/minspec/issues/1642), which snapshots
   owned-file blob shas at approve time).
3. Fail closed on its own absence: a missing witness on an ownership-declaring approved
   spec must not read as a passing one.
4. Reach adopters, or say plainly that it does not. `canonical.py` is scaffolded into
   adopter repos; `validate-frontmatter.ts` is not, so a validator-only control ships the
   basis change without its replacement.

### 3. Migration

DR-012 ([DR-012.md:143-148](DR-012.md)) already governs this: changing the hash basis
"silently invalidates every stored approval and must be migrated, not just edited".

**The proof needs no new field.** The new canonical form is the old one minus the
ownership lines, so the new hash's coverage is a strict subset of the old. A sidecar
fresh under the OLD hash therefore has content unchanged in every respect the new hash
could check, and re-stamping it asserts nothing the human did not already sign.

**A premise checked and discarded.** `baselineBlob` cannot serve as that proof. It is
SPEC-017 FR-4's **body-only** review baseline
([approval.ts:49-56](../../packages/minspec/src/lib/approval.ts#L49-L56)), minted from
bytes with all frontmatter excluded - structurally blind to the exact region the change
touches. A spec whose ownership lines were rewritten after approval would pass a
`baselineBlob` test and be laundered into `approved`.

**The half a naive migration would miss.** A sidecar that is stale *today because* someone
edited `implements:` becomes fresh the instant those lines leave the hash - status
improving from `stale` to `approved` with **no write, no marker and no human act**. A
migration that only inspects records it re-stamps cannot see this. Measured at
`2e9287d3`: **0 instances today**, and non-zero for any ownership edit landing before the
strip. So the migration must classify the **whole** sidecar tree under both bases before
writing anything, and quarantine - never silently keep - any record whose status would
improve. Quarantine means rewriting to a non-approving state, not deleting: deletion
destroys the `approvedBy`/`approvedAt` provenance the human did produce.

Required properties:

- **Classify before writing.** Both bases, every sidecar, no exceptions.
- **T0 invariant, migration-time:** no sidecar's resolved status improves from
  `stale`/`unapproved` to `approved` across the change. This is a pre-flight gate that
  fails the run, and it must be stated separately from any standing corpus invariant,
  because once the migration writes new-basis hashes a naive standing version of it fails
  on every re-stamped record by construction.
- **Frozen old canonicalizer.** The old hash must come from a vendored, fixture-pinned
  copy of the pre-change function, never the shipped module - otherwise the migration
  computes new hashes on both sides and accepts everything.
- **Provenance.** A re-stamp adds `rehashedAt` and `rehashedFrom` and does **not** touch
  `migrated`. Reusing `migrated: true` is forbidden twice over: it means "an approval the
  human never performed"
  ([approval.ts:43-46](../../packages/minspec/src/lib/approval.ts#L43-L46)), so it would
  libel genuine sign-offs, and DR-034's warn-to-error ratchet requires zero migrated
  records.
- **Idempotency**, matching the house precedent in `scripts/migrate-approvals.ts`: skip
  records already carrying `rehashedAt`, never overwrite `rehashedFrom`, and distinguish
  "already migrated" from "genuinely stale" in the refusal report.

### 4. Blast radius

Both canonicalizer twins change together -
[canonical.ts](../../packages/shared/src/canonical.ts),
[canonical.py](../../scripts/hooks/canonical.py) - plus the base64-embedded copy of the
Python one in `ci-review-templates.ts`, regenerated by `gen-ci-templates.mjs` and gated by
Rule 12 in `scripts/validate-frontmatter.ts`.

**What actually witnesses twin parity, stated precisely because the first draft got this
wrong.** Two cross-implementation tests run in CI, not one:
`packages/minspec/tests/canonical-parity.test.ts` (shells `canonical.py --hash` over every
spec) and `packages/minspec/tests/spec-gate.test.ts` (writes a Node-computed hash into a
sidecar and has the Python gate read it). Both are **corpus- or fixture-witnessed**, so
neither can catch a divergence no spec happens to contain. Two latent divergences are
already filed: [#1668](https://github.com/AIClarityAU/minspec/issues/1668) and
[#1669](https://github.com/AIClarityAU/minspec/issues/1669). Neither was executed by the
author of this record; they are cited as filed findings, not as verified here.

Any parity work this change relies on must run inside a harness CI already executes. The
Python golden suite is not one: `scripts/hooks/test_canonical.py` claims in its own
docstring to be "collected by CI alongside the Node suite" and is invoked by nothing
(#1669).

## Alternatives

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Leave `implements:` hashed, re-approve the 24** | 24 human spec re-reads now. Rejected by the founder on #1481. For an honest comparison: the path chosen instead is not free either - it is the §2 control (still undesigned) plus a migration touching 32 sidecars, with #1649 blocked throughout. |
| **`hashVersion` on the record instead of a re-stamp** | Zero re-stamps and zero human time - the only option with neither. Rejected for long-term simplicity, and that judgement is made without the prior one: this repo shipped record-versioning once and dropped it with no recorded rationale. |
| **Drift as a validator warning only** | Cheapest. Rejected on this record's own measurement: 140 non-fatal warnings already print under a passing banner. |

## Costly to Refactor

**Yes, high - which is why this is a DR and not a patch.** The hash is a content contract
embedded in 61 committed records and mirrored across two language implementations, one of
which is scaffolded into other people's repositories. Reversing this later means a second
corpus-wide re-stamp plus an adopter-visible hasher change, and by then the grandfathered
specs will carry ownership declarations no signature ever covered.

### Open question for acceptance

**How should ownership drift be made visible, given that arming the spec-gate does not
work?**

The recommendation put to the founder earlier - arm the gate - was withdrawn on
measurement: it reaches 2 of 24 specs and refuses nothing on ownership removal. Three
directions remain, and none is free.

- **(rec) Snapshot the owned set at approve time and gate on the union of the approved and
  current sets**, a sibling of #1642. It is the only direction that satisfies §2's
  constraint 2, because the freeze then draws on what the signature covered rather than on
  what the edit left behind.
  *Cost:* it is the largest of the three - a new sidecar field plumbed through the record
  type, its validator, its normalizer and both twins, plus a gate change - and it inherits
  the phase-band reach problem, so it still needs constraint 1 answered separately. It
  also puts a second digest on the Python gate's decision path, which creates a new
  twin-parity obligation exactly where §4 shows parity is only corpus-witnessed.
- **Widen the gate's phase band so ownership drift is evaluated regardless of phase.**
  Smallest change that fixes reach.
  *Cost:* it touches the gate's arming rule for every spec, not just drifted ones, so the
  blast radius is larger than the problem; and it does nothing about the removal
  direction, so it fixes the smaller half.
- **Accept that ownership drift is visible only in review, and say so in the record.**
  Zero build.
  *Cost:* it makes the honest statement that #1481 traded the alarm away rather than moved
  it, which is a materially different decision from the one the founder was offered - and
  it should be put back to them in those terms rather than settled here.
